## Questions

This is the first-year take-home quiz, rebuilt from the two Word documents that
used to live on the [equipment page](/information/information-for-new-folks/).
Answer each one with the definition **and** its rule-section-article citation,
the way the original asked for it — *What is a foul?* → **2-16-1**, a rule
infraction for which a penalty is prescribed.

### 1. The two pieces of equipment you will look at all night
What are the line-to-gain and down indicators, who supplies them, and where on
the field are they operated?

### 2. Shift
What is a shift, and what must the offense do after one?

### 3. Live ball
When does a ball become live?

### 4. Dead ball
What is a dead ball?

### 5. False start
What is a false start? Give all three ways it can happen. What happens if the
false start causes the defense to encroach?

### 6. Down
What is a down, when does it end, and what does "loss of down" actually cost a
team?

### 7. Fumble
What is a fumble?

### 8. Muff
What is a muff, and why does the difference between a fumble and a muff matter?

### 9. Neutral zone
What is the neutral zone on a scrimmage down, and what is it on a free-kick
down? When is it established?

### 10. Encroachment
What is encroachment, and over what interval can it occur?

### 11. How many on the line
How many Team A players must be on their line of scrimmage at the snap, and how
many may be backs?

## Answer Key

**1.** **2-26-5** defines the line to gain: the yard line established when a new
series is awarded, 10 yards in advance of the foremost point of the ball unless
a penalty follows the ready-for-play — and if it would extend into the end zone,
the goal line is the line to gain. **1-2-5** covers the equipment: either a
yardage chain joining two rods exactly 10 yards apart, or any other 10-yard
indicator with a visible line-to-gain indicator. The line-to-gain indicator
fixes the line to gain; the **down indicator marks the spot of the ball and
shows the number of the down**. Game management provides both, and the crew
checks them for accuracy before the game. They are operated about **2 yards
outside the sideline opposite the press box**, and the line-to-gain indicator
comes off the sideline when the line to gain is the goal line.
*Rules 1-2-5, 1-2-5a, 1-2-5b; 2-26-5*

**2.** **A shift is the action of one or more offensive players who, after a
huddle or after taking set positions, move to a new set position before the
ensuing snap.** After a huddle or shift, all players of A must come to an
absolute stop and remain stationary simultaneously — no movement of hands, feet,
head or body — for **at least one second** before the snap. A shift that
simulates action at the snap is a false start, not an illegal shift.
*Rules 2-39; 7-2-6; 7-1-7a*

**3.** **A live ball is a ball in play. It becomes live when the ball has been
legally snapped or free kicked and a down is in progress.**
*Rule 2-1-2*

**4.** **A dead ball is a ball not in play. The ball is dead during the interval
between downs.**
*Rule 2-1-1*

**5.** After the ball is ready for play and before the snap begins, no false
start may be made by any A player. **It is a false start if (a) a shift or
feigned charge simulates action at the snap; (b) any act is clearly intended to
cause B to encroach; or (c) any A player on his line between the snapper and the
player on the end of his line, after having placed a hand or hands on or near the
ground, moves his hand(s) or makes any quick movement.** It is a dead-ball foul,
5 yards. **If a false start causes B to encroach, only the false start is
penalized.**
*Rules 7-1-7, 7-1-8*

**6.** **A down is action which starts with a legal snap — beginning a scrimmage
down — or when the ball is kicked on a free kick, beginning a free-kick down. A
down ends when the ball next becomes dead.** **Loss of a down is the loss of the
right to replay a down** — it does not cost a team the ball, it costs them the
repeat.
*Rules 2-7-1, 2-7-2*

**7.** **A fumble is any loss of player possession other than by handing, passing
or legal kick.**
*Rule 2-18*

**8.** **A muff is the touching of a loose ball by a player in an unsuccessful
attempt to secure possession.** The difference is possession: **a fumble starts
from possession, a muff never had it.** It matters most on the 4-3-1 EXCEPTION,
which returns a ball fumbled out of bounds to the spot of the fumble — that
exception applies to a fumble and **not** to a muff.
*Rules 2-27, 2-18; 4-3-1 EXCEPTION*

**9.** **The neutral zone is the space between the two free-kick lines during a
free-kick down, and between the two scrimmage lines during a scrimmage down. On
a free-kick down it is 10 yards wide; on a scrimmage down it is as wide as the
length of the football. It is established when the ball is ready for play.**
After the snap it may be expanded up to 2 yards behind the defensive line of
scrimmage, inside the boundary lines — but on a scrimmage kick it is never
expanded into the end zone.
*Rules 2-28-1, 2-28-2*

**10.** **Encroachment occurs when a player is illegally in the neutral zone
during the time interval starting at the ready-for-play and until the ball is
snapped or free kicked**, as in 7-1-1, 7-1-5 or 7-1-6. An entering substitute is
not considered a player for encroachment restrictions until he is on his team's
side of the neutral zone. **Encroachment also occurs when a player violates the
free-kick restrictions** of 6-1-3a. It is a 5-yard penalty.
*Rules 2-8-1, 2-8-2; 7-1-5, 7-1-6; 6-1-3a*

**11.** **A scrimmage formation requires a minimum of five A players legally on
their line at the snap.** At the snap, at least five A players on their line must
be numbered 50-79, **no more than four A players may be backs**, and only one of
the players not on the line may penetrate the vertical plane through the
waistline of his nearest teammate who is on the line — the player under the
snapper, who is also considered a back.
*Rules 2-14-1; 7-2-5a, 7-2-5b; 7-2-3; 2-32-3*
