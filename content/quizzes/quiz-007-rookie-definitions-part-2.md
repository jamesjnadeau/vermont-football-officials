## Answer Key

**1.** **A lineman is any A player who is facing his opponent's goal line with
the line of his shoulders approximately parallel thereto, and with his head or
foot breaking an imaginary plane drawn parallel to the line of scrimmage through
the waist of the snapper when the ball is snapped.**
*Rule 2-32-9*

**2.** **A back is any A player who has no part of his body breaking the plane of
an imaginary line drawn parallel to the line of scrimmage through the waist of
the nearest teammate who is legally on the line** — except for the player under
the snapper, who is also considered a back.
*Rule 2-32-3*

**3.** **At least five A players legally on their line at the snap** (2-14-1),
**no more than four backs, and at least five of the players on the line numbered
50-79** (7-2-5a, 7-2-5b). **Of the players not on the line, only one may
penetrate the vertical plane through the waistline of his nearest teammate who is
on the line** — he must have his hands in position to receive a hand-to-hand
snap, though he is not required to receive it. Everyone else must be in legal
position as a back. Why the down matters: on first, second or third down, if A
sets or shifts into a scrimmage-kick formation, the snapper may wear 0-49 or
80-99, in which case A needs only **four** players numbered 50-79 on the line —
and that snapper is an ineligible receiver for the down unless B touches the
pass.
*Rules 7-2-3; 2-14-1; 7-2-5a, 7-2-5b and EXCEPTION 1*

**4.** **A forward pass is a pass thrown with its initial direction toward the
opponent's end line. A backward pass is a pass thrown with its initial direction
parallel with or toward the runner's end line.** It is the **initial direction**
that decides it, not where the ball comes down. If the potential passer is
contacted before he releases the ball and the ball is released, **it is a forward
pass if his arm was moving forward on contact.** A forward pass ends when it is
caught, touches the ground or goes out of bounds; a backward pass ends when it is
caught or recovered or goes out of bounds.
*Rules 2-31-2 and NOTE, 2-31-5, 2-31-4, 2-31-6*

**5.** Eligibility rules apply **only to a legal forward pass**. Eligible
receivers are **(a) all A players eligible by position and number — those who at
the time of the snap are on the ends of their scrimmage line or legally behind
the line (a possible total of six) and are numbered 0-49 or 80-99; (b) all A
players, once B touches a legal forward pass; and (c) all B players.**
*Rule 7-5-6*

**6.** **A catch is the act of establishing player possession of a live ball
which is in flight, and first contacting the ground inbounds while maintaining
possession** — or having the forward progress of the player in possession stopped
while the opponent is carrying the player who is in possession and inbounds.
Catching is always preceded by touching, so if the touching causes the ball to
become dead, securing possession has no significance. A simultaneous catch is one
in which opposing inbounds players have joint possession.
*Rules 2-4-1, 2-4-2, 2-4-3*

**7.** **An ineligible A player has illegally touched a forward pass if he bats,
muffs or catches a legal forward pass, unless the pass has first been touched by
B.** The penalty is **5 yards plus loss of down**.
*Rule 7-5-13 and PENALTY*

**8.** **It is a legal forward pass if, during a scrimmage down and before team
possession has changed, a player of A throws the ball with both feet of the
passer in or behind the neutral zone when the ball is released. Only one forward
pass may be thrown during the down.**
*Rule 7-5-1*

**9.** An illegal forward pass is a foul. The four kinds are **(a) a pass after
team possession has changed during the down — 5 yards; (b) a pass from beyond the
neutral zone — 5 yards plus loss of down; (c) a second or subsequent forward pass
thrown during a down — 5 yards plus loss of down; and (d) a pass intentionally
thrown into an area not occupied by an eligible offensive receiver, or thrown
incomplete to save loss of yardage or to conserve time — intentional grounding,
5 yards plus loss of down.** Two exceptions to (d): the spike, where a player
directly behind the snapper throws the ball forward to the ground immediately
after receiving a snap that was neither muffed nor touched the ground; and the
throwaway, where the passer has possessed the ball beyond the lateral boundary of
the free-blocking zone as established at the snap, the pass reaches the neutral
zone including its extension beyond the sideline, and the passer is the only
player to possess the ball after the snap ends.
*Rule 7-5-2 with EXCEPTIONS 1 and 2, and PENALTY; Table 7-5-2*

**10.** **A free kick is any kick which puts the ball in play to start a
free-kick down.** After the ready-for-play and before the kick, each player other
than the kicker and the holder for a place kick must be behind his free-kick
line. **A free kick is used for a kickoff, for a kick following a safety, and if
a free kick is chosen following a fair catch or awarded fair catch.** A punt may
not be used for a free kick other than after a safety.
*Rules 2-24-3; 6-1-2*

**11.** After the ball is marked ready for play and until the ball is kicked:
**(a) no player other than the kicker and the holder for a place kick may be
beyond his free-kick line; and (b) no K player other than the kicker may be more
than 5 yards behind K's free-kick line.** In addition, **at the time the ball is
kicked, at least four K players must be on each side of the kicker**, and all R
players must be behind their free-kick line. Violating the free-kick
restrictions is encroachment — 5 yards.
*Rules 6-1-3a, 6-1-3b, 6-1-4; 2-14-3; 2-8-2*

**12.** **A scrimmage kick is any kick from in or behind the neutral zone during
a scrimmage down.** A place kick, punt or drop kick may be used; for a place kick
the ball must be controlled on the ground or on a legal kicking tee by a
teammate. K may kick only from in or behind the neutral zone and only before team
possession has changed; **R may not punt, drop kick or place kick.**
*Rules 2-24-4; 6-2-1*

**13.** Careful — this is the definition of the **formation**, not a requirement
for kicking. **A scrimmage-kick formation is one in which no player is in
position to receive a hand-to-hand snap from between the snapper's legs, and at
the snap either (a) a player is in position with a knee on the ground 7 yards or
more behind the line of scrimmage, in position to be the holder and receive the
long snap, with another player 3 yards or less behind him in position to attempt
a place kick; or (b) a player is 10 yards or more behind the line of scrimmage
and in position to receive the long snap.** Being in this formation is what
unlocks the numbering exceptions — **it is not necessary to be in a
scrimmage-kick formation to execute a legal scrimmage kick.**
*Rules 2-14-2a, 2-14-2b; 6-2-1; 7-2-5b EXCEPTIONS*

**14.** **Any receiver may catch or recover a kick in the field of play and
advance** — a free kick unless any R player has given a valid or invalid
fair-catch signal, and a scrimmage kick unless it is during a try or any R player
has given such a signal. R may catch or recover in K's end zone. **For the
kicking team it depends on where the ball is: any K player may catch or recover a
scrimmage kick while it is in or behind the neutral zone and advance, unless it
is during a try.** Beyond the neutral zone, K may catch or recover only if the
kick was first touched by a receiver clearly beyond the neutral zone — and that
catch or recovery **causes the ball to become dead**. If any K player recovers or
catches a **free** kick, the ball becomes dead.
*Rules 6-2-2, 6-2-3, 6-2-4; 6-1-5, 6-1-6*

**15.** **A fair catch is a catch by a receiver of a free kick in or beyond the
neutral zone to the receiver's goal line, or of a scrimmage kick beyond the
neutral zone to the receiver's goal line, after a valid signal, under conditions
in which the receiver forfeits the right to advance the ball in return for
protection from being blocked or tackled by an opponent.** **A valid fair-catch
signal is the extending and lateral waving of one arm, at full arm's length above
the head, by any R player.** **Only the receiver who gives a valid signal is
afforded protection** — if a teammate makes the catch instead it is not a fair
catch, but the ball is dead. No receiver may advance the ball after a valid or
invalid signal has been given by any R player.
*Rules 2-9-1, 2-9-3; 6-5-2, 6-5-3, 6-5-5*
