If the forecast has thunder or lightning in it, settle the plan before kickoff
rather than in the middle of it.

## Before kickoff

Prior to kickoff the Referee should ask the game site administrator where both
teams will go in the case of thunder or lightning, and pass that answer along to
both head coaches. Do it at the coaches' meeting, while everyone is calm and
listening.

## Stopping the game

It is the responsibility of the officials on the field to stop the game **with
the first sound of thunder or the first flash of lightning**. Not after the
second one, and not after somebody checks a phone.

## The 30-minute rule

The VPA sets this one, and it is stricter than instinct:

> When thunder is heard or lightning is seen, students and spectators must leave
> the field and go inside.
>
> Once lightning has been recognized or thunder heard, teams must wait **at
> least thirty minutes** before resuming activities. Any subsequent lightning or
> thunder after the beginning of the 30-minute count **resets the clock**, and
> another count begins.
>
> — VPA Athletic Policies, High School, "VPA Lightning Rule"

Two things officials get wrong about it:

**The count restarts on every new sighting or sound**, regardless of where you
think the storm is or which way you think it is moving. A distant rumble at
minute 28 buys another 30 minutes. Do not negotiate this with a coach who wants
to finish.

**The field has to clear** — players, coaches, and spectators go inside, not to
the sideline. That part is the site administrator's to enforce, but the crew is
usually the reason it starts happening.

Somebody on the crew owns the clock and says the time out loud. In a normal
crew that is the Line Judge, who is already the crew's timing specialist.

## Who decides whether to resume or call it

Officials, coaches, and administrators talk it through, and **the final decision
belongs to the game administrator** — the same person the VPA Football Guide
puts in charge of suspending and resuming a delayed game, and who is expected to
weigh all the factors including input from the visiting administrator.

The crew's job is to give that decision good information: how much game is left,
what the field is doing, how many resets the count has already taken, and what
the rule book allows. Then support whatever they decide.

**If the game is suspended after pregame activities have started, the crew
receives a full fee plus mileage.** The cancellation cases are on
[Vermont Rules & Policies](/information/vermont-rules-and-policies/), along with
the rest of what the host administrator owns — see also
[Game Day Administration](/information/game-day-administration/).

## The rule book

Rule 3, Article 5 covers suspending and resuming a game and is worth reading
before a night that looks like this one will be. It explains the thought
process; the VPA lightning rule sets the floor Vermont will not go below.
