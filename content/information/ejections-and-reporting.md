A disqualification is the one call on a Friday night that keeps going after the
game ends. In Vermont every varsity ejection is reviewed by name, in writing, by
Monday noon — so what the crew does in the ten minutes after the flag decides
whether that review has anything to work with.

The rule book tells you when someone is disqualified. This page is about the
doing, and about what happens next.

## On the field

1. **Be sure, then be quick.** Get the crew together if you need to, but do not
   hold a conference the whole stadium watches. Once the crew agrees, act.
2. **The Referee reports it**, gives the disqualification signal, and tells the
   **head coach** directly — by number, by act, and in one sentence. Not the
   assistant, not the player.
3. **The player leaves.** Under NFHS rules a disqualified player goes to the
   team box under supervision or leaves the field entirely, per the school's
   arrangement. Settle with the coach and the site administrator where that
   player will be for the rest of the game.
4. **Get it on the card** before the next snap: number, team, quarter, clock
   time, and the rule.
5. **Agree the wording as a crew** before the ball is next snapped, while all
   five of you still have the same picture. What everyone reports afterward
   should match because it was true, not because it was rehearsed — but agreeing
   on what happened is not rehearsing.
6. **Then get back to the game.** The next series after an ejection is when
   crews lose games. Slow it down and be visible.

## Write it down before you leave

Do it in the locker room, not in the car and not tomorrow. Twenty minutes of
recall is worth more than any amount of reconstruction:

- Number, name if you have it, team, and role — player, coach, or bench
  personnel
- Quarter and clock time
- The act, described physically. What the body did, not what you concluded from
  it.
- The rule and the signal used
- Which official saw it, and from where
- **Exact words**, if there were words. Quote them. Do not clean them up, and do
  not paraphrase profanity into something milder — the review committee needs
  what was actually said.
- Whether the coach was told, by whom, and what the coach said back
- Anything the player did after the ejection

Write what you saw. Do not write what you inferred about intent, and do not
write an argument for your own call.

## The Vermont review workflow

This is what makes Vermont different from the rule book:

> All varsity game ejections arising from penalties assessed for conduct during
> play where ejection is provided by the rule shall be reviewed by **both VFOA
> commissioners and the VFOA rules interpreter** to confirm the proper
> application of the rule. A final determination by the VFOA will be delivered
> to the VPA and the VIFL Executive Secretary **no later than noon on the Monday
> following the game** in which the ejection occurred.
>
> — 2026 VPA Football Guide, §III.3

Three things follow from that for a crew:

**It is a review of the rule, not of your judgement.** The stated purpose is to
confirm the rule was applied properly. A determination that the rule was
misapplied is not a finding that the crew was dishonest.

**The window is short.** Two commissioners and the rules interpreter have to
read, discuss, and deliver a determination before noon Monday. A report that
lands Monday morning has effectively arrived too late to be discussed. Report
the same night.

**Judgement calls stand.** Under VPA policy, an official's judgement in ordering
an ejection is final. The only grounds for appealing the resulting suspension
are gross misconduct by the official, or a written report from the official
stating that the ejection was erroneous. If you believe you got it wrong, that
written report is the mechanism — use it, promptly.

### The part this page cannot yet tell you

**Who the crew notifies, on what form, and by when** is a VFOA-internal
procedure and is not in the VPA guide. Ask your commissioner for the current
answer before your first varsity game and do not improvise one on a Sunday
night. It is recorded as an open item in
[the source register](https://github.com/jamesjnadeau/vermont-football-officials/blob/master/docs/sources.md),
and this page will be updated when it is answered.

## What happens next

**The suspension.** A student or coach ejected for unsportsmanlike conduct or a
flagrant foul is suspended from the next contest in that sport. Football is
explicitly a one-contest sport for this purpose, where most VPA sports carry a
two-contest suspension. A suspended student may not be in uniform; a suspended
coach may not attend the contest.

**A second ejection in the same season** suspends that student or coach for the
remainder of the season in that sport.

**An ejection in the last contest of a season** carries into the next sport the
student participates in. If it is the last contest of their last season, the
school imposes proportionate discipline itself and reports it to the VPA.

**Enforcement is the school's.** If a suspended student or coach is allowed to
participate, forfeiture of the contest is mandatory.

**Team accumulation.** In the 2026 season, any team — coach, principal, or
athletic director — that accumulates **more than two ejections** is required to
meet with the VPA Activities Standards Committee before entering or continuing
in the tournament. The threshold is set annually; check the current guide rather
than quoting this number in a later season.

**Assault on an official** is a separate and much heavier category. A coach or a
team member who intentionally strikes, shoves, kicks, or makes other aggressive
physical contact with an official before, during, or after a contest is
**disqualified immediately** and may be suspended for up to one full calendar
year, pending a hearing before the Activities Standards Committee. "Member of
the team" includes managers, scorekeepers, timers, and statisticians. If this
happens, the report you write that night is the record the hearing will run on.

## Coach conduct and complaints about officiating

Coaches are expected to keep concerns about officiating off the sideline and out
of the press. The VPA's coaches' code says a coach should not make derogatory
statements to the press, booster clubs, or others about officiating, officials,
or other schools, and that serious concerns travel through channels: school
administration to the officials' association, or to the Executive Director of
the VIFL, or to the administration of the schools involved.

That is worth knowing on the field. A coach who is told there is a formal route
is being told something true, and it is a better answer than arguing the point
at the 35.

**Protests** work the same way. Any protest about the game itself is settled by
the Referee on the field. A principal who wants to appeal a ruling on the
grounds that a rule was misapplied has 24 hours to contact the VPA office. No
protest is resolved by the crew after the game.

## Incident reports that are not ejections

Write one the same night for anything that could turn into a phone call:

- A fight or near-fight that did not rise to a disqualification
- A spectator problem the administrator was told about
- A facility or safety issue that was reported and not fixed
- Anything a school failed to provide — see
  [Game Day Administration](/information/game-day-administration/)
- Any injury that stopped the game for an extended period
- Any conversation with a coach that you would not want summarized by someone
  else first

Send it to your commissioner. The standing advice is simple: if you are
wondering whether it needs a report, it needs a report, and it needs it tonight.
