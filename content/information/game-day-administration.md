This is the ninety-minutes-out page: what the host school owes the crew, what
the crew walks and checks, and who to find when something is missing.

Almost none of it is your job to fix. Most of it is your job to notice, say out
loud to the right person, and write down if it does not get fixed.

## What the host school must provide

The VPA Football Guide makes these the host school's responsibility, not a
courtesy:

- A **field properly equipped**
- At least **one game football**
- A **yardage chain** and a **down marker crew**
- A **clock operator**
- **Staff to handle the crowd**
- At least one **identifiable administrator present**
- **Emergency medical services**

The chain crew and the clock operator are held to a standard, not just a
headcount: each school must provide a full, competent crew with serviceable
equipment, and the clock operator is expected to know football rules, signals,
and game administration well enough to run the timing system properly.

**When something is missing**, find the site administrator, say what is missing
and what it will mean, and give them a chance to solve it before kickoff. A
short chain crew or a missing ambulance is an administrator's problem to
resolve; the crew's job is to make sure they know about it early enough to act,
and to record it if they do not.

## The field and equipment walk

Walk it as a crew, not alone. Things worth settling before anyone is in a
hurry:

| What | What you are looking for |
| --- | --- |
| Goal posts and pylons | In place, padded where they should be, pylons at all eight spots |
| Field markings | Yard lines and hash marks legible, goal lines and end lines clear |
| Team box and restricted area | Marked, and long enough for the sideline you are about to manage |
| Sideline barrier | The guide recommends 7–10 yards between the field and spectators |
| Game balls | At least one legal ball, properly inflated; ask what else is available |
| Timing system | Test it with the operator, and agree what happens if it fails |
| Lighting and surface | Anything that is a safety problem rather than an inconvenience |

**Delay-of-game problems versus report-it-and-play problems.** If a condition
makes the game unsafe or unplayable — no medical coverage, an unmarked goal
line, a hazard on the surface — it is settled before kickoff, and the game
waits. If a condition is wrong but not dangerous — a short chain crew, a faded
restricted-area line, an unpadded post that can be worked around — you play,
you tell the administrator, and you write it down the same night rather than
relitigating it at halftime.

## Briefing the chain crew and the clock operator

**The clock operator meets the crew 30 minutes before kickoff.** In practice the
Line Judge owns that meeting. Cover: setting and testing the clock, how the crew
will reach them, halftime length, what happens if the clock fails, and — if the
game looks lopsided — the [point differential running
clock](/information/vermont-rules-and-policies/). Hand them the
[Clock Officials Cheat Sheet](/information/clock-officials-cheat-sheet/) if they
have never done it before.

**Remember the scoreboard rule.** A field scoreboard clock in use is the
official clock. If it is not being used as the official clock, it is turned off
— there is no such thing in Vermont as a scoreboard running unofficially in the
background.

**Chain crew**, at the pregame: where to stand and how far back, that they take
their instructions only from the Linesman, that they move only on the signal,
and how a measurement is handled. Tell them to freeze on any whistle they are
unsure about.

**Names.** The site administrator, clock operator, and scorer are identified to
the Referee by rule. Get the names and where each of them will be.

## The coaches' and captains' meetings

The times are fixed by the guide:

| Time before kickoff | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 30 minutes | Officials meet the **home** team |
| 25 minutes | Officials meet the **visiting** team |
| 15 minutes | Team and player introductions |
| 10 minutes | Coin toss |
| 5 minutes | National Anthem |

**Only captains may be on the field** for pregame and halftime meetings with
officials. After introductions the players leave the field and return to the
bench; only the captains come back out for the toss.

At the coaches' meeting, get the legal-equipment certification, ask about any
**equipment waiver** the school has been granted — schools must notify both
opponents and officials — and ask about anything unusual: a trick formation
they want checked, an injured player, a first-time kicker.

## Playoff and championship differences

- The **higher seed wears the dark or colored jersey and is the home team**.
- The **scoreboard clock is official**.
- The chain gang and down marker are operated by the host school, unless the
  higher seed exercises its option to bring its own crew. Either way they wear
  vests or other apparel that makes them easily discernible to the officials.
- Each team provides **two ball persons** to assist the officials.
- Locker rooms open two hours before game time.
- Sideline access is restricted to team personnel and people the school has
  specifically authorized; security has been instructed to remove anyone else.
- Practice at the neutral championship site before pregame warm-up is
  prohibited.
- The point differential running clock applies in postseason games, championship
  games included.

## When a game is suspended

**The final decision on suspending and resuming a delayed game belongs to the
host administrator**, who is expected to consider all the factors and to try to
reach the visiting administrator for input first. The crew advises. The crew
does not decide.

What the crew owes that decision is information: how much of the game is left,
what the field is doing, whether the delay has been long enough that restarting
is its own risk, and what the rule book allows. Then it is the administrator's
call and the crew supports it.

If the game is suspended after pregame activities have started, the crew
receives a full fee plus mileage. The cancellation cases are on
[Vermont Rules & Policies](/information/vermont-rules-and-policies/).

For the lightning rule that triggers most Vermont suspensions, see
[Foul Weather Procedures](/information/foul-weather-procedures/).

## Before you leave

Settle anything that has to be reported while the crew is still together and
still remembers it: ejections (see
[Ejections & Reporting](/information/ejections-and-reporting/)), injuries that
required a stoppage, anything a school failed to provide, and any incident in
the stands that the administrator was told about. Agree who writes what, and
write it that night.
