Vermont plays NFHS football. The playing rules published by the National
Federation are the standard for every game in this state, and nothing on this
page changes one of them.

What this page carries is the layer on top: what the Vermont Principals'
Association adds, restricts, or decides for itself in the annual **VPA Football
Guide**. Almost none of it is in the rule book, and an official who has read the
rule book cover to cover still does not know it. For the NFHS baseline, see the
[Cliff Notes](/information/football-rules-summary/).

## The point differential running clock

This is the one to know cold. It comes up on a Friday night with a scoreboard
nobody is watching carefully, and getting it wrong changes how long the game
lasts.

### When it goes on

| Situation | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| First three quarters, differential reaches **35 or more** | A running clock **may** be used — but only by mutual agreement of both coaches *and* the Referee. Any one of the three can decline. |
| Third quarter, differential reaches **42 or more** | A running clock **shall** be used, and stays on for the rest of the game even if the trailing team closes the gap back under 35. |
| Once it is running in the fourth quarter | It stays on for the balance of the contest, even if the deficit drops below 35. |

The clock starts on the snap, or on the legal touch of a free kick, on the first
play after the differential is reached.

The rule applies in the postseason too, championship games included.

### What no longer stops it

Once the running clock is in effect, the game clock **keeps running** through
all of these — things that would stop it in a normal game:

- A first down awarded to either team, including after a change of possession
- The ball or the runner going out of bounds
- An incomplete forward pass, legal or illegal
- A touchback
- An inadvertent whistle
- Every penalty enforcement

### What still stops it

| Stoppage |
| --- |
| The end of a period |
| Any score — touchdown, try, field goal, safety |
| A free kick awarded following a fair catch |
| A charged team timeout |
| A coach–referee conference |
| An official's timeout — injury, equipment, a first down measurement, anything else required |

### How it restarts

After any of those stoppages, the clock starts again when the ball is next
**marked ready for play**, or when it is **legally touched on the free kick**
following a score, a fair catch, or an awarded fair catch.

Tell the clock operator before the game that this rule exists and what the
signal will be. They are usually a volunteer, and the running clock is the
single most confusing thing that can happen to them —
the [Clock Officials Cheat Sheet](/information/clock-officials-cheat-sheet/)
covers it from their side of the table.

## Sub-varsity differences

**Sub-varsity quarters are 12 minutes** — the same length as varsity — unless
the two schools mutually agree to shorten them. That agreement is common and it
is legal, but it is theirs to make, not the crew's. Ask at the pregame what the
two schools agreed to; do not assume the schedule told you.

**Overtime is capped at two innings each.** One inning is four downs for a team
from the ten-yard line, so a sub-varsity game that is still tied after each team
has had two possession series ends tied. Varsity games use the NFHS tiebreaker
without a Vermont cap, and championship ties are broken by the procedure in the
NFHS rule book.

## The pregame timeline

The guide sets the sequence, and the same one is used for championship games:

| Time before kickoff | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 30 minutes | Officials meet with the **home** team |
| 25 minutes | Officials meet with the **visiting** team |
| 15 minutes | Team and player introductions |
| 10 minutes | Coin toss |
| 5 minutes | National Anthem |

**Only captains are allowed on the field** for the pregame and halftime meetings
with officials. After introductions, players leave the field and return to the
bench area; only the captains come back out for the toss. The suggested protocol
is that each school hands the crew a protocol sheet on arrival, before its team
takes the field.

## The clock, the scoreboard, and the table

The **site administrator, clock operator, and scorer are identified to the
Referee**. Get their names in the pregame; you will need at least two of them
before the night is over.

**When a field scoreboard clock is used it is official.** If it is not being
used as the official clock, it is turned off — Vermont does not allow a
scoreboard running unofficially in the background where a bench can read it and
argue from it.

The **clock operator meets the game officials 30 minutes before kickoff**. The
guide expects that operator to have a working knowledge of football rules,
signals, and game administration, and to be proficient with the timing system in
use. Chain crews are held to the same standard: each school must provide a full,
competent crew and serviceable equipment.

## The play clock

**The 2026 VPA Football Guide does not mention a play clock at all.** It sets no
Vermont procedure for one, adopts none, and prohibits none.

So the play clock is whatever NFHS says it is, kept on the field by an official
in the ordinary way. Do not expect a visible play clock, and do not plan
mechanics around one. If a particular stadium has one, settle in the pregame
who operates it and what happens when it disagrees with the field count — that
is a local arrangement, not a state rule.

This is an open question rather than a settled one: the guide's silence is
recorded in [the source register](https://github.com/jamesjnadeau/vermont-football-officials/blob/master/docs/sources.md),
and the rules interpreter is the person to ask.

## Fees, cancellations, and mileage

**2026 officials' fees:**

| Level | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Varsity | $97.50 |
| JV | $75.00 |

All game officials' fees in the VPA tournament are paid by the VPA.

**When a game does not get played:**

| Situation | What the crew is owed |
| --- | --- |
| Game **suspended** after pregame activities have started | Full fee plus mileage |
| Game **cancelled** with adequate prior notice — 2 hours or more | No fee |
| Game **cancelled** without adequate notice, officials present | Half a game fee plus mileage |

The guide does not set a mileage rate; see
[Getting Assigned](/information/getting-assigned/) for how the VFOA handles
mileage claims in practice.

**Who decides to suspend.** The final decision on suspending and resuming a
delayed game belongs to the **host administrator**, who is expected to weigh all
the factors and to try to reach the visiting administrator for input first. It
is not the Referee's call, and it is not the home coach's. See
[Foul Weather Procedures](/information/foul-weather-procedures/) for the
lightning rule that most often triggers it.

## Equipment waivers

When a school has been granted an equipment waiver, it must notify **both its
opponents and the officials**. If you are told about one at the pregame, get the
specifics: which player, what equipment, and what the waiver actually permits.
If a school mentions a waiver you were never told about, note it and play — then
report it afterward. Nothing about a waiver is decided on the field.

Schools are also expected to tell opponents their home and away jersey colors
before the season and to send a roster of possible players to every scheduled
school.

## Points of emphasis for 2026

1. Sportsmanship
2. Targeting defenseless players
3. Legal uniforms and equipment

The NFHS rules book carries the full treatment of each. The VPA reprints them so
that coaches and officials arrive at week 1 emphasizing the same three things.

## Other things the guide fixes

- **Eight regular-season games maximum**, excluding the championship events.
  Week 9 is the first round of the playoffs.
- **32 quarters maximum** per player for the season, overtime excluded. Varsity
  starters are ineligible for sub-varsity games.
- **Drones are prohibited** at all VPA-sanctioned football games — spectators,
  media, and third parties alike. Violators are asked to stop immediately and
  may be removed.
- **Only authorized personnel on the sidelines.** Schools are responsible for
  who is in the team area, and event security has been instructed to remove
  anyone not on the team or authorized by the school.

## What this page is not

The VPA Football Guide and the NFHS rule book govern. This is a summary kept by
one official for the convenience of other officials, and it is only as current
as the date in the footnote above. When a coach quotes something at you that
contradicts this page, the guide wins — and then tell whoever maintains this
site, so the page gets fixed.
