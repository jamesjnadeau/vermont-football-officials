Everything the crew settles before the toss, everything each of you owes the
field before kickoff, and what the crew owes the game afterwards. The white hat
runs the conference; everybody answers.

## The crew conference

*There is no magic formula. There is a list.*

### Work down it

- **Rules changes and level differences.** Especially early in the season, and
  especially if someone on the crew also works small college or youth ball.
- **Special coverage.** Hurry-up. Obvious onside. Lightning suspension. A fight.
  Decide how the crew handles each before it happens.
- **Making the call.** Which crew signals you use — receiver off the line, two
  sticks, the player counts and what "enough / too many / too few" looks like.
  Shared coverage means eye contact, and everyone officiates the dead ball.
- **Reporting fouls.** The Referee says how he wants them. Offence and defence,
  or jersey colour? Include the status of the ball at the foul and the result of
  the play. Wings tell the coach who, what, and any special enforcement.
- **Time-outs.** Who times it, where everyone stands, how the teams are told it
  is over, and who tells each coach how many they have left — after every one.
- **Bench decorum.** Agree what is out of line, so two officials on the same crew
  don't have two standards. Wings find the get-back coach.
- **An injured official.** Generally a crew of four carries on without a Line
  Judge and a crew of five without a Back Judge. Down to two: one works as
  Referee, one as Head Line Judge.
- **Halftime.** Do you let the teams leave first? The recommendation: meet at a
  designated spot, let them go, Referee signals the timer to start the clock.
  Settle who notifies the teams and the crew of the time remaining.
- **Leaving the field.** Together, or on your own.
- **Game expectations.** Any history between these two schools that will show up
  tonight.

## Meeting the head coaches

*Referee plus one — usually the Umpire. Home coach first.*

### What he must do

- Verify **verbally** that his players are legally equipped and will use no
  illegal equipment.
- Acknowledge that everyone is expected to show good sportsmanship.
- Identify any player with a protective guard, cast or brace that needs
  inspecting.
- Name the representative who will **request time-outs** from the sideline. That
  person stays the same all game except in an emergency.
- Name the representative — coach or player — who will **decide penalty
  acceptance or declination**.

### What you ask him

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Get-back coach** | The assistant who keeps players and staff out of the coaching belt. Get his name. |
| **Captains** | Numbers, and names if you can. A captain called by name helps you calm a teammate later. |
| **Toss** | What he wants if he wins it. If the captain then says something different, ask "are you sure that's what you want?" |
| **Quarterback** | Right- or left-handed. The Referee's position on every scrimmage down depends on the answer. |
| **Kickers** | Right- or left-footed, punter and place kicker both. Same reason. |
| **Trick plays** | Unusual formations or plays either team plans. Foreknowledge means it gets officiated correctly. |
| **Medical** | Where the trainer will be. Whether a lightning detector is available and how weather will be communicated. |

### What you tell him

- When the toss is (normally 3 minutes before the game, unless your association
  says otherwise).
- That he is responsible for having his team on the field for the mandatory
  **3-minute warm-up** after halftime.
- Any planned extended halftime.

Introduce the crew and hand him a card with everyone's names on it. Avoid
conversation about what the other team does.

## On the field, 30 minutes out

*The crew takes the field together, in uniform.*

### Who does what

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **R** | Walk both sidelines and both end lines. Spot-check players warming up — tinted eyeshields, knotted jerseys, decorated towels. Obtain and approve the game balls. Then check the crew: has the Umpire inspected the tapings, has the Head Line Judge met the chain crew, has the Line Judge met the timer and ball helpers. |
| **U** | Inspect player equipment, casts, braces and tapings. Spot-check the warm-up the same way the Referee does. |
| **HL** | Inspect the down indicator and chains — securely attached, no kinks, ends properly covered. **No tape at the chain's midway point? Get some from a trainer and mark it.** Meet the line-to-gain crew. |
| **LJ** | Walk both sidelines and both end lines. Goalposts straight, undecorated, pads fastened, pylons placed. Meet the line-to-gain crew. In a crew of 4, also keep the game balls and meet the ball helpers and the timer. |
| **BJ** | Keep the game balls — gauge them if they reach the locker room, inspect by hand if they arrive on the field, and **mark the approved ones with your initials** so only those get used. Meet the ball helpers and the timer. |

Hazards — potholes, broken glass — go to game management to be fixed
immediately. If the field is lined for another sport, make sure the crew knows
which lines are football.

## The last few minutes

*Duties done. Now watch.*

The Referee, Head Line Judge and Back Judge move to midfield on the **Head Line
Judge's side**; the Umpire and Line Judge to midfield on the opposite side. Watch
both teams warm up without looking like you are inspecting them, and trade what
you see before the kickoff.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Kickers** | How strong are the legs? Does he slice it? How is the wind affecting the flight and the spin? |
| **Formations** | Double wide, slot, trips? Unusually wide splits? Do they rush the snap after getting set? |
| **Blocking** | Do the linemen block low? Is the tight end or slot back in the free-blocking zone? |
| **Defence** | Are the linebackers tight enough to be in the free-blocking zone at the snap? |
| **Receivers** | What patterns do they run? |

Warm up yourself — run the sideline, and stretch out of bounds near the end zone
if you need to. Keep pregame chat with players, coaches and spectators to a
minimum; it reads as favouritism.

**Professionalism.** No alcohol or tobacco, and no impairment by any controlled
or illicit substance, from arrival at the site until you leave after the game.

## How long your authority lasts

*Jurisdiction, p. 217.*

Your authority runs **through the Referee's declaration of the end of the fourth
period or overtime**. It ends — and the final score is approved — when **all**
game officials have left the visual confines of the playing area. Leave together
and nobody is left alone with it.

What survives that is **clerical** authority: you keep it through the completion
of any report responsive to something that happened while you had jurisdiction,
disqualifications included. A state association may still intercede over an
unusual incident before, during or after your jurisdiction, or where a game was
terminated before the end of regulation.

## After the game

*Postgame, pp. 217–219.*

### Owed to somebody else

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Disqualification** | Fighting, or two unsportsmanlike fouls on one person. The **Referee** contacts the conference officiating coordinator, and the report must say **at what point of the game** it happened. Many state associations want notice of poor sportsmanship as well. |
| **Reports** | Complete them in the locker room and send them immediately. |
| **Clock trouble** | A breakdown in cooperation with either clock operator, or either clock malfunctioning, goes to the state association or the assigning agency — reported by the official in charge of that timer. Timers are told to report crews back, so this runs both ways. |
| **Disqualified coach** | Out of sight and sound of the stadium for the rest of the game, and no coaching activity after the ejection. |

### The crew's own review

Relax first — before the shower, after it, or at dinner; do whatever suits the
crew. Then work down these:

- **Strange plays and rulings.** Confirm anything odd against the rules book and
  the case book, and confirm every unusual enforcement was handled properly. Did
  you take too long presenting options, or not long enough?
- **Points of emphasis.** Rough play, sportsmanship, dead-ball situations — were
  they handled? If not, what do you do differently next week?
- **Sideline decorum.** Did you let the coaches go too far? Were you
  approachable? Did they get penalty, time-out and clock information in time?
- **Tempo.** Did the game come to you, or did you assert yourself when you didn't
  need to? Did it find a flow?
- **Criticism, asked for and given.** "How'd I do?" expecting praise is not a
  question. Ask what you could have done better, take the answer, and be ready
  to give three things back to every crewmate. Working with someone new is the
  best chance you get: ask what their regular crew does differently.
