*What the tie-breaker actually does, and what it looks like on the field when neither team will lose.*

---

## 1. The procedure, in brief

Regulation ends tied. Before anyone leaves the field, the referee gathers both captains for a 3-minute intermission and a coin toss. From there:

- **Each team gets a series, first-and-goal from the 10-yard line** (15 in six-player), snapped from any hash-mark spot at the same end of the field, both teams attacking the same goal.
- **The line to gain is always the goal line.** There's no such thing as a first down in overtime — you either score or you don't.
- A field-goal attempt is legal on any down.
- **Each team gets one time-out per overtime period** — separate from, and unrelated to, however many they had left at the end of regulation.
- A series ends the instant the offense scores, turns the ball over, or fails on fourth down.
- **A defensive touchdown or safety ends the game immediately** — the defense doesn't need a second series to "confirm" it. An offensive safety puts 2 points on the board and sends the *other* team's series to the 10, not the usual free-kick.
- After each period, compare scores. Still tied? Play another period. Nothing about the procedure changes except who gets first choice.

### The coin toss and the alternating options

The **first overtime** toss works like a normal coin toss: winner chooses to go on offense, defense, or which goal to defend (though the goal is fixed by field conditions here, so it's really offense-or-defense, and the loser gets what's left).

**Every overtime after the first reverses the previous period's choice**, and the options keep alternating from there:

| Period | Who chooses first | What they typically pick |
|---|---|---|
| 1st OT | Winner of the coin toss | Offense or defense |
| 2nd OT | **Loser** of the original toss | Offense or defense (whichever they didn't get in 1st OT is now theirs to choose) |
| 3rd OT | Alternates back | ... and so on, each period, for as long as the game goes |

Officials don't re-flip the coin every period — the option holder is already determined by who chose what the period before. The referee announces it at the 2-minute intermission between periods so both benches know coming in.

## 2. Walk-through: a game that won't end

Here's how a stubborn tie actually plays out on the field, period by period.

**Regulation ends 21–21.** Home and Visitor are dead even. Whistle, both teams to their sidelines, referee summons both captains to midfield for the overtime coin toss.

### Overtime 1

Visitor wins the toss and elects **defense first** — a common choice, since knowing the number you need to beat is an advantage.

- Home: first-and-goal from the 10. A screen pass goes for 6 — touchdown. Home kicks the extra point. **Home 28, Visitor 21.**
- Visitor: first-and-goal from the 10. A run up the middle scores. Down two, Visitor goes for two to tie rather than kick to trail by one — the two-point try succeeds. **28–28.**

Still tied. Second overtime.

### Overtime 2

Because Visitor chose defense in OT1, **Home now gets first choice** — the option reverses. Home elects to go on **offense first** this time (Home's coach wants to force Visitor to know the number they need again).

- Home: first-and-goal from the 10. This series stalls — three incompletions and a fourth-down field goal try that's **blocked**. No points. Visitor's ball, but the series is over; possession changing on a failed field goal doesn't give Visitor a live return in this context — the down is simply over and Visitor's series begins fresh from the 10, same as any other overtime series.
- Visitor: first-and-goal from the 10, needing only a field goal to win outright. A false start backs them up to the 15 — this is where "first-and-goal from the 10" quietly becomes "first-and-goal from wherever accepted penalties put you," since normal foul enforcement still applies inside the overtime series. Visitor's kicker lines up on 4th down and **misses** the field goal.

Still tied, 28–28, after two overtimes.

### Overtime 3

Options reverse again — Visitor gets first choice this time.

- Visitor: offense first. A long completion to the 2, then a fumble at the goal line that **Home recovers in its own end zone** — a touchback for Home, not a defensive score, since the ball became dead there with Home in possession. Visitor's series ends with nothing.
- Home: first-and-goal from the 10. On 2nd down, Home's back breaks a run to the 1... but is ruled down before the plane. 3rd-and-goal from the 1 — stuffed. 4th-and-goal — Home elects the **field goal** rather than risk it, and it's good. **Home 31, Visitor 28.**

Home wins after three overtime periods.

That's the shape of it: nothing about the rules changes between periods 1 and 11 if a game somehow goes that long. Each period is a clean, self-contained pair of series from the 10, and the game simply keeps adding periods — with the choice of offense/defense alternating and the score reset to "who's ahead after both teams have had this period's series" — until one team is ahead when both series are over.

## 3. Scenario: roughing the kicker on a game-deciding field goal

Kicking fouls get extra scrutiny in overtime because there's no "next series" to push a penalty into — every series is winner-take-all, so where the crew enforces the foul changes who gets another set of downs, or whether the game is over at all.

**Missed kick, roughing called.** Visitor lines up for a game-winning field goal, 4th-and-goal from the 8. The kick sails wide, but as the kicker plants his foot, a Home lineman who was blocked into him keeps driving and rides him to the ground well after the ball is gone — **roughing the kicker**, not the lesser running-into-the-kicker foul, because the contact was forcible rather than incidental.

- A missed kick would end Visitor's series with nothing. Accepting the penalty is obviously better, so Visitor takes it.
- Roughing the kicker is 15 yards **and an automatic first down**, enforced from the **previous spot** — the spot of the snap.
- Because the line to gain in overtime is always the goal line, the automatic first down doesn't add any distance to cover — it simply means Visitor's series isn't over. They get a fresh set of downs at the enforced spot instead of the ball going over on downs.
- Had the contact been the lesser **running into the kicker** foul instead (5 yards, no automatic first down), the same result-of-the-play comparison still favors taking the penalty over the missed kick — but Visitor only gains 5 yards and is still facing a numbered down, not a fresh series, since that foul carries no automatic first down.

**Successful kick, roughing called anyway.** Same play, different outcome: the kick is good, and it's the go-ahead score. Home still gets flagged for roughing the kicker on the follow-through.

- The successful kick is the better outcome for Visitor, so the score counts — a team never has to give up a good result to take a penalty.
- Roughing on a successful kick is enforced like a foul on a try: from the **succeeding spot**. If the game isn't over, the *other* team's next series starts 15 yards further back than it otherwise would have.
- If that field goal was the second team's series of the period and it broke the tie, the game ends the instant the ball is dead. The roughing foul becomes a note for the game report and for the disqualification/ejection review, not something with anywhere left to be enforced — there's no more football left to play.

None of this is a special "overtime rule." It's the same result-of-the-play test that governs every foul on a kick in regulation — it just matters more here, because the down it happens on might be the last one of the game.

## 4. Things that trip up a crew mid-overtime

- **Timeouts don't carry over.** A team that used all three of its second-half timeouts still gets one full timeout in the first overtime, another fresh one in the second, and so on.
- **Fouls between the periods** (dead-ball or unsportsmanlike fouls in the intermission) are enforced on the next series under the same succeeding-spot logic as any other dead-ball foul — see the enforcement guide for the general rule; overtime doesn't have a special exception.
- **A disqualified player** from a fight at the end of regulation or during an overtime intermission still sits out the overtime series his team is disqualified for, and the penalty is enforced from wherever that team's series would otherwise begin.
- **PSK (post-scrimmage kick) enforcement does not apply in overtime.** A foul by the receiving team during a scrimmage kick in a regulation series can sometimes be enforced from the previous spot with an option to take the result of the kick; that option goes away in overtime, since there's no "next series" to protect.
- **A safety is not the same as a defensive touchdown.** An offensive team that snaps the ball out of its own end zone and is tackled there gives up 2 points and its opponent gets to start *their* series from the 10 as normal — the game does not end. Only a score that actually crosses the goal line the other way (or an actual touchdown by the defense) ends the game immediately.
- **Overtime rules are state-adopted, not universal NFHS mandate.** The NFHS publishes RESOLVING TIED GAMES as a *model* procedure; state associations can adopt it as written, modify it (some states use a shorter yard line, some cap the number of periods and go to a kick-off contest, some allow ties in the regular season), or reject it. Vermont's association rules govern; confirm the current adopted procedure at your pregame conference, and say so out loud to both coaches so nobody is surprised when the coin gets flipped a second or third time.

## 5. Quick reference

| | |
|---|---|
| Starting spot | 10-yard line (15 in six-player), any hash-mark position |
| Line to gain | The goal line, every down |
| Field goal | Legal on any down |
| Timeouts | One per team, per overtime period |
| Series ends on | Score, turnover on downs, or change of possession |
| Game ends on | Any score that leaves one team ahead once both series are complete, or immediately on a defensive touchdown/safety |
| Still tied after a period? | Play another; the offense/defense choice reverses each period |
| PSK enforcement | Does not apply |
