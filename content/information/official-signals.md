## Why the numbers matter

Every PENALTY statement in the rulebook names its signal. Rule 9-3 ends like this:

> PENALTY: Art. 2 – Blocking Below the Waist – **(S40)** – 15 yards; Art. 3 – Blocking After Signal – **(S43)** – 15 yards; Art. 4 – Blocking Kicker or Place-Kick Holder – **(S30)** – 15 yards; Art. 5 – Blocking in the Back – **(S43)** – 10 yards; Art. 6 – Chop Block **(S41)** – 15 yards; Art. 6 – Clipping – **(S39)** – 15 yards; Art. 7 – Illegal Block – **(S43)**, live ball foul, 10 yards.

Learn the numbers and the rulebook tells you what to signal without a second lookup. It also means a crew can talk in shorthand — "thirty-eight, forty-five, offense" is unambiguous.

**On the illustrations:** the drawings on this page are our own stick figures, one per signal and numbered to match the chart, so `S38-24` in the rules book points at the same two pictures here. They are original drawings of the *motions* — the NFHS chart's own artwork is copyright Referee and is neither reproduced nor traced. Each drawing carries the chart's caption and a one-line note on the motion, and a red arrow where the signal moves.

Where a drawing and the printed chart disagree, the chart wins. It is on pages 96–97 of your rules book, and on the [official signals PDF at nfhs.org](https://nfhs.org/resources/sports/football-officials-signals-2026).

## 1. Who signals

None of the mechanics in this section come from the rules book — they are officials'-manual practice and crew convention, and they vary by association. Confirm them in pregame.

Only the referee signals fouls to the press box. Everyone else *reports* — the covering official gives the referee the foul, the number, and the team, and then goes to explain it to their coach. Signaling from the wing is standard in college and the NFL and wrong here, because a second official may have a different foul on the same snap.

Other officials do give the administrative signals in their own areas: stop-the-clock, touchdown, incomplete pass, and so on.

**Mechanics for the white hat:**

- Come to a complete stop before you start. Don't walk through your signals.
- Face the press box. Every foul signal, every time.
- Pick a spot about halfway up the stands and keep your head up.
- No facial expression. You are neither pleased nor annoyed.
- Don't jab the arm on the point to the offending team.
- Stand up straight. Clipping and blocking below the waist require bending at the waist; almost nothing else does — including penalty declined.
- You don't need to be between the hashes. Clear of players is enough.

## 2. How the codes are built

Signal numbers chain together, and the chains follow three consistent patterns.

### S7 is the dead-ball prefix

When the foul happened while the ball was dead, the dead-ball signal comes **first**, then the foul.

| Code | Meaning |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `S7-18` | dead ball, then encroachment (6-1-3a, 7-1-1) |
| `S7-19` | dead ball, then false start / snap infraction / free-kick infraction (7-1-2, 7-1-7) |
| `S7-21` | dead ball, then delay of game (3-6-2, 3-6-3, 3-6-4) |
| `S7-22` | dead ball, then illegal substitution — the dead-ball version of the foul (3-7) |
| `S7-23` | dead ball, then disconcerting act (7-1-9) |
| `S7-27` | dead ball, then unsportsmanlike conduct (9-5, 9-8-1) |

Note the pair in Rule 3-7: illegal substitution is `S7-22` when it's a dead-ball foul and plain `S22` when it's a live-ball or nonplayer foul. The prefix carries real information.

## 3. Signal sequences

**Live-ball foul — four steps:**

1. The foul
2. Point toward the offending team
3. The next down, or the new-series signal
4. Ready-for-play, or start-the-clock — whistle at the same time

**Dead-ball foul — four or five steps:**

1. `S7`, the dead-ball signal
2. The foul
3. Point toward the offending team
4. The next down, or the new-series signal
5. Ready-for-play, or start-the-clock

Four when the penalty is declined: dead ball, the foul, the team, the declination, then the ready — no down signal, because the down has not changed. The officials' manual works the example: Team R is flagged for encroachment before a try and Team K declines because the kicker likes the distance he has.

**The dead-ball signal is not optional on a false start or encroachment.** Those two are always preceded by it (§2.2).

Add loss of down (`S9`), automatic first down (`S8`), or disqualification (`S47`) where the chain calls for it. Disqualification goes after the point to the offending team and before the down.

Self-evident fouls — false start, delay of game, encroachment — don't need a preliminary signal and then a second one after enforcement.

Wait for the head line judge to confirm the chains are set before the ready-for-play.

Two of these carry rules weight worth knowing cold:

**First touching** is not a foul. It happens when a K player touches a scrimmage kick beyond the neutral zone before any R player touches it, and it gives R the option of taking the ball at that spot. There is no first touching in R's end zone — the ball is dead when it breaks the plane. R loses the privilege if R fouls after touching the kick, or if any penalty in the down is accepted.

**Disregard flag** is the referee's signal alone. If you've thrown a flag you're no longer sure about, tell the referee — but only the referee waves it off.

## 6. Learning order

If you're starting from zero, this is the order that pays off fastest.

**Week one — the ones you give every drive.** `S1` ready for play, `S2` start clock, `S3` stop clock, `S8` first down, `S10` incomplete pass, `S5` touchdown, `S6` safety, `S14` end of period.

**Week two — the pre-snap fouls.** `S18` encroachment, `S19` false start and illegal formation, `S20` shift and motion, `S21` delay, `S22` substitution. These are most of what you'll actually signal in a normal game, and they're all self-evident to the crowd, which takes some pressure off.

**Week three — the personal-foul family.** `S38` and its modifiers: `S38-24`, `S38-25`, `S38-26`, `S38-45`, `S38-46`, `S38-47`. Learning `S38` as an umbrella rather than six unrelated signals cuts the memorization roughly in half.

**Week four — blocking and holding.** `S42` holding, `S43` illegal use of hands, `S39` clipping, `S40` below the waist, `S41` chop block, `S44` helping the runner.

**Week five — pass and kick.** `S33` interference, `S34` roughing the passer, `S35`/`S36`/`S37` passing fouls, `S30`/`S31`/`S32` kicking fouls.

**Throughout — the prefixes and suffixes.** `S7` in front for dead ball, `S8` or `S9` at the end. These are worth more than any individual foul signal because they apply everywhere.
