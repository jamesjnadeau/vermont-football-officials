## Why the numbers matter

Every PENALTY statement in the rulebook names its signal. Rule 9-3 ends like this:

> PENALTY: Art. 2 – Blocking Below the Waist – **(S40)** – 15 yards; Art. 3 – Blocking After Signal – **(S43)** – 15 yards; Art. 4 – Blocking Kicker or Place-Kick Holder – **(S30)** – 15 yards; Art. 5 – Blocking in the Back – **(S43)** – 10 yards; Art. 6 – Chop Block **(S41)** – 15 yards; Art. 6 – Clipping – **(S39)** – 15 yards; Art. 7 – Illegal Block – **(S43)**, live ball foul, 10 yards.

Learn the numbers and the rulebook tells you what to signal without a second lookup. It also means a crew can talk in shorthand — "thirty-eight, forty-five, offense" is unambiguous.

**On the illustrations:** the NFHS signal chart drawings are NFHS copyright and are not reproduced here. The descriptions below are of the motions; for the pictures, use the chart on pages 96–97 of your rules book or the [official signals PDF on nfhs.org](https://nfhs.org/resources/sports/football-officials-signals-2025).

## 1. Who signals

None of the mechanics in this section come from the rules book — they are officials'-manual practice and crew convention, and they vary by association. Confirm them in pregame.

Only the referee signals fouls to the press box. Everyone else *reports* — the covering official gives the referee the foul, the number, and the team, and then goes to explain it to their coach. Signaling from the wing is standard in college and the NFL and wrong here, because a second official may have a different foul on the same snap.

Other officials do give the administrative signals in their own areas: stop-the-clock, touchdown, incomplete pass, and so on.

**Mechanics for the white hat:**

- Come to a complete stop before you start. Don't walk through your signals.
- Face the press box. Every foul signal, every time.
- Pick a spot about halfway up the stands and keep your head up.
- No facial expression. You are neither pleased nor annoyed.
- Don't jab the arm on the point to the offending team.
- Stand up straight. Clipping and blocking below the waist require bending at the waist; almost nothing else does — including penalty declined.
- You don't need to be between the hashes. Clear of players is enough.

## 2. How the codes are built

Signal numbers chain together, and the chains follow three consistent patterns.

### S7 is the dead-ball prefix

When the foul happened while the ball was dead, the dead-ball signal comes **first**, then the foul.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `S7-18` | dead ball, then encroachment (6-1-3a, 7-1-1) |
| `S7-19` | dead ball, then false start / snap infraction / free-kick infraction (7-1-2, 7-1-7) |
| `S7-21` | dead ball, then delay of game (3-6-2, 3-6-3, 3-6-4) |
| `S7-22` | dead ball, then illegal substitution — the dead-ball version of the foul (3-7) |
| `S7-23` | dead ball, then disconcerting act (7-1-9) |
| `S7-27` | dead ball, then unsportsmanlike conduct (9-5, 9-8-1) |
| `S7-29` | dead ball, then sideline interference (9-8-1k) |

Note the pair in Rule 3-7: illegal substitution is `S7-22` when it's a dead-ball foul and plain `S22` when it's a live-ball or nonplayer foul. The prefix carries real information.

### S38 is the personal-foul umbrella

Most contact fouls are the personal foul signal followed by a modifier that says which one.

| Code | Foul | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `S38` | personal foul, no further specification — charging an opponent out of the play, hurdling, contact with a helmet-less opponent, forceful contact on a defenseless receiver, slapping a blocker's head | 9-4-3, 9-4-3d, 9-4-3p, 9-4-7 |
| `S38-24` | illegal helmet contact — butt block, face tackle, spear, targeting, contact against a defenseless player | 9-4-3i, 9-4-3m |
| `S38-25` | horse-collar | 9-4-3 |
| `S38-26` | illegal blindside block | 9-4-3n |
| `S38-29` | illegal personal contact in the restricted area | 9-4-8 |
| `S38-45` | grasping the face mask, helmet opening, chin strap, or attached tooth and mouth protector | 9-4-3 |
| `S38-46` | tripping | 9-4-3o |
| `S38-47` | personal foul with disqualification — fighting, contacting an official, striking/kicking/kneeing | 9-4-1, 9-4-2, 9-4-3j |

### S8 and S9 are suffixes

They go at the **end**, after the foul and the team.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `S35-9` | illegal forward pass, also loss of down (7-5-2) |
| `S36-9` | intentional grounding, also loss of down (7-5-2d) |
| `S16-9` | illegal touching, also loss of down (7-5-13) |
| `S38-34-8` | personal foul, roughing the passer, automatic first down (9-4-4) |
| `S38-30-8` | personal foul, roughing the kicker or holder, automatic first down (9-4-5) |
| `S38-8` | personal foul, roughing the snapper, automatic first down (9-4-6) |

Roughing the passer and roughing the kicker carry the full chain — umbrella, specific foul, first down — while roughing the snapper has no signal of its own and is simply `S38-8`. Running into the kicker is `S30` alone: 5 yards, no first down.

### A few chains worth memorizing whole

| Code | Situation | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `S15` | first nonplayer offense — **warning only**, no yardage | 9-8-3 |
| `S7-29` | second offense — 5 yards | 9-8-3 |
| `S7-29-27` | third and subsequent — 15 yards | 9-8-1k, 9-8-3 |
| `S27-47` | second unsportsmanlike by the same individual, or a sub leaving the box during a fight — 15 and disqualification | 9-5, 9-8-1l |

## 3. Signal sequences

**Live-ball foul — four steps:**

1. The foul
2. Point toward the offending team
3. The next down, or the new-series signal
4. Ready-for-play, or start-the-clock — whistle at the same time

**Dead-ball foul — five steps:**

1. `S7`, the dead-ball signal
2. The foul
3. Point toward the offending team
4. The next down, or the new-series signal
5. Ready-for-play, or start-the-clock

Add loss of down (`S9`), automatic first down (`S8`), or disqualification (`S47`) where the chain calls for it. A declination is signaled after the point to the offending team.

Self-evident fouls — false start, delay of game, encroachment — don't need a preliminary signal and then a second one after enforcement.

Wait for the linesman to confirm the chains are set before the ready-for-play.

Numbers and coverage are taken from the 2025 rules book. **The motion descriptions should be checked against the printed chart before you rely on them** — the chart is the authority on how each signal looks.

### Ball status, down, and warnings

| No. | Signal | When you give it | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S7** | Dead ball | Prefix for any foul that occurred while the ball was dead. The rules book shows the contrast directly: illegal substitution is `S7-22` as a dead-ball foul and `S22` as a live-ball foul. | 3-7, 9-5, 9-8 |
| **S8** | First down | The new-series signal, and the suffix marking an automatic first down. The number comes from the chart; the rules book uses it only as the trailing digit on the three roughing fouls, which are exactly the three automatic first downs in 10-1-7. | 10-1-7 |

## 5. Timing and administrative signals

These are the signals you'll give most often, and the rules book doesn't reference them by number in its PENALTY statements — they appear only on the chart itself. **Get the numbers from pages 96–97**; the functions are:

- Ball ready for play
- Start the clock
- Stop the clock / time-out
- Discretionary or injury time-out
- TV / radio time-out
- Reset the play clock to 25 seconds
- Reset the play clock to 40 seconds
- Touchdown, field goal, point(s) after touchdown
- Safety
- Touchback
- Incomplete forward pass / penalty declined / no play / no score / toss option deferred
- Legal touching of a forward pass or scrimmage kick
- First touching
- Inadvertent whistle
- Disregard flag
- End of period
- Untimed down

Two of these carry rules weight worth knowing cold:

**First touching** is not a foul. It happens when a K player touches a scrimmage kick beyond the neutral zone before any R player touches it, and it gives R the option of taking the ball at that spot. There is no first touching in R's end zone — the ball is dead when it breaks the plane. R loses the privilege if R fouls after touching the kick, or if any penalty in the down is accepted.

**Disregard flag** is the referee's signal alone. If you've thrown a flag you're no longer sure about, tell the referee — but only the referee waves it off.

## 6. Learning order

If you're starting from zero, this is the order that pays off fastest.

**Week one — the ones you give every drive.** Ready for play, start clock, stop clock, first down (`S8`), incomplete pass, touchdown, safety, end of period.

**Week two — the pre-snap fouls.** `S18` encroachment, `S19` false start and illegal formation, `S20` shift and motion, `S21` delay, `S22` substitution. These are most of what you'll actually signal in a normal game, and they're all self-evident to the crowd, which takes some pressure off.

**Week three — the personal-foul family.** `S38` and its modifiers: `S38-24`, `S38-25`, `S38-26`, `S38-45`, `S38-46`, `S38-47`. Learning `S38` as an umbrella rather than six unrelated signals cuts the memorization roughly in half.

**Week four — blocking and holding.** `S42` holding, `S43` illegal use of hands, `S39` clipping, `S40` below the waist, `S41` chop block, `S44` helping the runner.

**Week five — pass and kick.** `S33` interference, `S34` roughing the passer, `S35`/`S36`/`S37` passing fouls, `S30`/`S31`/`S32` kicking fouls.

**Throughout — the prefixes and suffixes.** `S7` in front for dead ball, `S8` or `S9` at the end. These are worth more than any individual foul signal because they apply everywhere.

## Sources and currency

Signal numbers, coverage, and rule references above come from the **2025 NFHS Football Rules Book** — the Penalty Summary on pages 94–95 and the PENALTY statements in Rules 3 through 10.

Two caveats about what is and isn't sourced. Most signal numbers appear in the book only inside a compound code — `S24`, for instance, is never printed alone, only as `S38-24`. The individual meanings given above are consistent with every PENALTY statement in the book, but the chart is what actually assigns them. And **the motion descriptions and everything in Section 1 come from officials'-manual practice, not from the rules book.**

Before the season, check:

- **The printed chart, pages 96–97**, for the illustrations and for the timing and administrative signal numbers.
- **The current year's rules changes**, which occasionally alter a signal or renumber the chart.
- **Vermont adaptations**, which are the binding interpretation for our games.
