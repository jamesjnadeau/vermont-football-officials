How games reach you, how to give one back, and how you get paid. New officials
need this and so does everyone else.

## Who assigns games

The association's leadership manages assignments. Games are matched to officials
by the level they are ready for — if you are ready for JV you will be assigned
JV, and the same for varsity.

Questions about a specific assignment go through the association's leadership.
If you do not know who to ask, the [contact page](/contact/) will get you to
someone who does.

## The platform

Assignments run through **Arbiter**. Your account is created as part of
registration, along with the background check, so there is nothing separate to
set up.

Arbiter is where you will do all four of these:

- Keep your availability current
- Accept and decline games
- See your schedule
- Collect your payments

## Submitting availability

**Keep your availability up to date in Arbiter.** There is no hard deadline, but
have it in **at least a week before the season starts** — you cannot be assigned
to dates the assignor cannot see you are free for.

Update it during the season as things change, rather than accepting a game and
giving it back later. That is the whole trick to being easy to assign.

## Accepting and declining

You accept or decline games through Arbiter, and the expectation is that you do
it **as quickly as you can**. An assignor working through a schedule is blocked
on your answer; a game you sit on for a week is a game that could have gone to
someone else with no fuss.

Declining a game you cannot work is not a mark against you. Sitting on it is.

## Turnbacks

Giving back a game you already accepted is the one place a new official can
quietly damage their standing without realising it. The rule is simple: **act
professionally and tell people through the proper channels, as early as you
can.** A turnback three weeks out is an administrative task. The same turnback
on Thursday night is someone else's problem to solve at speed.

The association's own position is worth quoting, because it is more forgiving
than most:

> We understand people are human and make mistakes. Do all you can to remedy
> problems you create and you'll be fine.

So: if you have to give a game back, say so immediately, say it to the right
person, and help fix it. What damages standing is silence, not the turnback.

## Crews

Crews are formed around the needs of the game and the compatibility of the
people on them. Crews are **rotated week to week** on purpose, so that everyone
becomes familiar with working alongside everyone else — you should expect to
work with most of the association over a season rather than settling into one
fixed crew.

## Getting paid

| Game | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| Varsity | **$97.50** |
| Junior varsity | **$75.00** |

These are the 2026 rates set by the VPA Football Guide, which also fixes what
you are owed when a game does not get played: a full fee plus mileage if the
game is suspended after pregame activity has started, nothing if it is cancelled
with two hours' notice or more, and half a fee plus mileage if it is cancelled
without adequate notice and you were there. The full schedule is on
[Vermont Rules & Policies](/information/vermont-rules-and-policies/).

**Payment** runs through Arbiter, and you withdraw it by whichever of the
available methods suits you.

**Mileage** is claimed on a form listing your mileage and the game it was for.
It is reimbursed at the standard IRS mileage rate, paid back at the end of the
season. After your first year, your membership dues are usually taken out of
that mileage reimbursement rather than billed separately.

**Taxes:** Arbiter holds the 1099. This site does not give tax advice — talk to
someone who does if you need it.

## Playoffs

Playoff fees are paid by the VPA rather than by the host school. The
[Season Calendar](/information/season-calendar/) carries this year's pairing,
semifinal and championship dates.

Playoff assignments lean on seniority. That said, the association deliberately
tries to give newer officials a playoff game each year where it can, so the
experience is shared rather than reserved for the same crews every season.

## Meetings

Attendance matters. There are three or four meetings before the season, at least
one of them in person, and presence is expected. During the season most meetings
are virtual.
