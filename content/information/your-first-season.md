You have registered and you are on the schedule. This page is what to do
between now and your first game, and what to do after it.

If you have not registered yet, start at
[Becoming a Football Official](/information/becoming-an-official/).

## Before your first game

- Registration complete and the background check cleared.
- Concussion training done on NFHS Learn.
- [Equipment](/information/information-for-new-folks/) bought or borrowed.
- Availability entered in Arbiter.
- Preseason meetings attended — three or four, at least one in person.
- You have talked to at least one experienced official who knows you are new.

## The rule book

Your rule book comes with your membership, in **both print and digital form** —  
you do not need to buy one. The NFHS also publishes them directly:

- [https://nfhs.org/](https://nfhs.org/)

Veteran officials quote rules from memory with the rule number attached. That is
not where you start, and nobody expects it in your first year — but it is worth
knowing what you are working toward, because being able to cite the rule ends
arguments faster than being right does.

## What to study, in what order

The site has ten reference cards and a rules summary, which is a lot to face
with no entry point. This is the order that works.

**1. The rules, in outline — a couple of evenings.**
Read [Cliff Notes — the NFHS Rules Book](/information/football-rules-summary/)
straight through once without trying to memorise it. You are building a map of
where things live, not learning the rules yet. Come back to it constantly.

**2. Your position — this is the one that matters most.**
Most new officials work as a wing, so start with
[Head Line Judge](/information/head-line-judge-position-card/) and
[Line Judge](/information/line-judge-position-card/). Learn your pre-snap
position, your keys, and what you have on each kind of play. Read the card for
whichever spot you are actually assigned. The others —
[Referee](/information/referee-position-card/),
[Umpire](/information/umpire-position-card/),
[Back Judge](/information/back-judge-position-card/) — are worth reading later
so you know what your crewmates are doing.

**3. Between downs — an hour, and the highest-value hour on this list.**
[Between Downs](/information/between-downs-crew-card/) covers what happens after
the whistle and before the snap. New officials lose the game here, not on
spectacular plays: getting the ball spotted, the chains set, and everyone ready.

**4. Run and pass plays — the bulk of what you will see.**
[Run & Pass Plays](/information/run-pass-plays-crew-card/). Most snaps in most
games are one of these.

**5. Kicking plays — every game has them and they go wrong fastest.**
[Kicking Plays](/information/kicking-plays-crew-card/).

**6. The clock, then fouls.**
[Clock & Timing](/information/clock-timing-crew-card/) and then
[Fouls & Enforcement](/information/fouls-enforcement-crew-card/). Enforcement
is the one thing you can genuinely defer — your white hat will walk you through
it, and you will learn it fastest by watching it happen.

Two more when you have room:
[7-Man Mechanics](/information/7-man-mechanics/) if you ever work a larger crew,
and [Recommended Reading](/information/recommend-reading/).

## The quizzes

The [quizzes](/quizzes/) are the fastest way to find out what you do not know
yet. Start with the two rookie sets, which are the association's own first-year
take-home quizzes:

- [Quiz 006 — Rookie Definitions, Part 1](/quizzes/quiz-006-rookie-definitions-part-1/)
- [Quiz 007 — Rookie Definitions, Part 2](/quizzes/quiz-007-rookie-definitions-part-2/)

Answer them out loud with the rule, section and article. The remaining quizzes
are expert-level case plays — save those for your second year, or work them with
your crew.

There is **no rules exam** to pass. The association uses quizzes as a group
activity to build knowledge, not to grade you.

## Your first game

**Arrive early** — early enough for a full pregame with your crew, not early
enough to just make kickoff. Ask your white hat when they want you there and
then beat it.

**Bring** your uniform, flag, beanbag, whistle, down indicator, a pen, and your
rule book. Bring a spare of anything small enough to lose.

**In pregame**, say out loud that it is your first game, or one of your first.
Nobody will hold it against you and everyone will adjust to help you. Listen for
your keys, your responsibilities on kicks, and what your crew wants on
measurements and on the goal line.

**When you do not know a rule mid-game — and you will not — talk to your crew.**
Kill the play, get together, get it right. That is the whole answer. A crew
conference that takes thirty seconds and produces the correct ruling is a good
outcome. Guessing quickly is the only actual mistake available to you here.

## After the game

- Write down every situation you were unsure about, while it is fresh. That list
is your study plan for the week.
- **Ask your white hat for feedback, specifically.** "How did I do?" gets you
"fine". "Was I in the right spot on the sweep to my side?" gets you something
you can use.
- Look up the rules behind whatever you wrote down, and read them properly, not
just the summary.

## Working with a mentor

The association has experienced officials who will go out of their way to help
you learn — this is a team effort in the same way the game is. There is no
formal sign-up. Ask someone whose work you noticed, or ask the director to point
you at someone.

The officials who get good fastest are the ones who ask questions all season, not
the ones who wait to be corrected.

## Moving up

You are assigned to the level you are ready for. What determines that is your
knowledge of the mechanics, your knowledge of the rules, and your character on
the field — not the number of seasons you have served. When you are ready for
JV, you will be assigned JV; the same is true of varsity.

Playoff assignments lean on seniority, but the association deliberately tries to
give newer officials playoff experience each year rather than reserving it.

How assignments actually reach you is on
[Getting Assigned](/information/getting-assigned/).
