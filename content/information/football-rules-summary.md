# Cliff Notes — 2025 NFHS Football Rules Book

A condensed study guide to the full rules book. 

Rule references
(`9-4-3m`) are rule-section-article and match the source text; each heading links
to the chapter file with the actual language. 

**This is a study aid, not an
authority** — for any ruling that matters, read the rule itself, and remember
that your state association is the only binding interpreter of these rules.

---

## 1. The game in one minute

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Object | Advance the ball across the opponent's goal line, or kick it through their goal. Most points wins. |
| Teams | 11 players each (may continue with fewer if out of substitutes) |
| Field | 360 × 160 ft; end zones 10 yards deep; hash marks 53'4" from each sideline |
| Goal | Crossbar 10 ft high, uprights 23'4" apart, extending ≥10 ft above the crossbar |
| Length | Four 12-minute periods (48 minutes), 1 minute between periods, 10–20 minute halftime (15 normal) + 3-minute mandatory warm-up |
| Series | Four downs to reach the line to gain, normally 10 yards |
| Scoring | Touchdown 6 · Field goal 3 · Safety 2 (to the opponent) · Try 2 (touchdown) or 1 (field goal/safety) · Forfeit 1–0 |
| Authority | Game officials take charge 30 minutes before kickoff (or earlier by state rule) through the referee's declaration of the end of the game, plus clerical authority for reports. Referee rules on anything not covered; protests of NFHS rules are not recognized. |

Team designations used throughout: 
- **A** = team snapping the ball
- **B** = itsopponent; 
- **K** = kicking team, 
- **R** = receiving team. A1/B1/K1/R1 are individual players.

## 2. What changed for 2025

From COMMENTS ON THE 2025 RULES CHANGES:

| Rule | Change |
| --- | --- |
| 4-3-1 EXC, 3-4-2d, 8-5-2a EXC | **Forward fumble out of bounds returns to the spot of the fumble** (between the goal lines) — closes an unfair-advantage loophole. Also affects the momentum exception. |
| 1-5-3c(2) | Electronic equipment may relay plays from the sideline with **fixed signs, non-audio methods and still images**; in-helmet communication and video for players between the numbers remain illegal. |
| 1-5-3c(3) NEW | Players may not wear **any audio or video device** to record or transmit. |
| 1-5-1d(5)a NEW | *Effective 2026*: no attachments on tooth and mouth protectors that serve no protective purpose or create a health risk. |
| 1-5-2d NEW | *Effective 2027*: **arm sleeves** manufactured to enhance contact must meet the SFIA specification and carry the seal (aligns with the glove rule). |
| 9-6 PENALTY, 10-4-4b deleted | Continued cleanup of **Rule 10-4 penalty enforcement**, especially illegal participation. |
| Six-player Rules 2 & 7 | A fumbled snap may be advanced once possession changes from the snap receiver to another offensive player. |

Editorially, forceful contact against a **defenseless receiver** moved from Rule 2
(definitions) to **9-4-3p** (conduct), so the foul is defined by the contact, not
by the tackler's technique.

**2025 Points of Emphasis:** illegal and improperly worn equipment (escalating
options: replace the player for a down → delay of game → unsportsmanlike on the
head coach); sportsmanship (penalize prolonged, choreographed, opponent-directed
acts without warning — allow brief spontaneous emotion); defenseless
players/targeting (responsibility rests with the player initiating contact; when
in doubt, flag it).

## 3. Rule-by-rule cliff notes

### Rule 1 — The Game, Field, Players and Equipment

- **Field:** 4-inch lines; restraining line ≥2 yards outside the sidelines/end
  lines; team boxes between the 25-yard lines with a 2-yard coaches' area that is
  **restricted while the ball is live**; 3-yard try line centered ≥24 inches;
  pylons 4 inches square, 18 inches high at goal-line/end-line/hash-mark
  intersections. Logos may not obscure required markings.
- **Ball:** tan, pebbled or approved composition, 14–15 oz, inflated 12½–13½ psi,
  1-inch white/yellow stripes; **NFHS authenticating mark required for 9th grade
  and above**. Referee judges legality; any official may order it changed.
- **Player designations:** field captain is the only player who may talk to
  officials; numbers 0–99 (leading zero illegal); the head coach names a
  **penalty-decision representative** for the whole game.
- **Mandatory equipment:** NOCSAE helmet with 4-point chin strap and warning
  label; jersey covering pads and tucked in (visitors white body, home dark);
  numbers ≥8" front / ≥10" back; hip/tailbone, knee, shoulder, thigh pads; single
  tooth-and-mouth protector; pants covering knees; legal shoes/cleats.
- **Illegal equipment** includes jewelry (religious/medical items taped and worn
  under the uniform), tinted eyeshields, ball-colored items, hard braces without
  ½-inch closed-cell padding, uncovered back/rib pads, tear-away jerseys,
  slippery substances, non-conforming towels, play cards not on the wrist/arm, and
  **anything not worn as the manufacturer intended**.
- **Pregame verification:** the head coach certifies to the referee *and* another
  official that all players are legally equipped. Missing/improperly worn
  equipment → official's time-out and the player is **replaced for at least one
  down** (repairs under 25 seconds excepted).
- Coaches may use electronic devices; communicating electronically with a *player*
  is legal only during an outside 9-yard mark conference.
- **Table 1-7** lists the 16 state-association adoptions (officials' count, ball
  mandate, instant replay in postseason, overtime procedure, point differential,
  halftime length, TV/radio time-outs, small-sided field dimensions, …).

### Rule 2 — Definitions of Playing Terms

The vocabulary chapter — 45 sections. The ones that decide plays:

- **Catch** = possession of a ball in flight + first contact with the ground
  inbounds (or forward progress stopped while being carried by an opponent).
  **Catching is always preceded by touching**, so if the touch made the ball dead,
  later possession is meaningless.
- **Blocking:** closed-hand and open-hand techniques defined (hands inside the
  frame, at or below the shoulders, in advance of the elbow). **Blocking below the
  waist** = initial contact below the waist from the front or side (opponent with a
  foot on the ground). **Chop block** = two teammates, one high one low.
  **Blindside block** = block on an opponent who does not see it coming.
  **Clipping** = from behind at or below the waist; **block in the back** = inside
  the shoulders, below the helmet, above the waist.
- **Free-blocking zone:** 4 yards either side of the snap, 3 yards behind each
  line. Below-the-waist blocks require *all* involved players on the line and in
  the zone at the snap, contact in the zone, immediate action. The zone
  **disintegrates once the ball leaves it**.
- **Defenseless player (2-32-16):** especially vulnerable by position and focus —
  passer, receiver attempting a catch, the intended receiver during/just after an
  interception, a runner in the grasp with progress stopped, a kick returner, a
  player on the ground or sliding feet-first, a player out of the play, a player
  taking a blindside block with forceful contact not initiated with open hands.
  **When in question, a player is defenseless.**
- **Illegal helmet contact** = butt blocking, face tackling, spearing.
  **Targeting** = taking aim and initiating contact above the shoulders with
  helmet, forearm, hand, fist, elbow or shoulder.
- **Kicks:** free kick, scrimmage kick, place kick, drop kick, punt, illegal kick,
  **pop-up kick** (illegal). A kick ends when a player gains possession or the ball
  becomes dead.
- **Fouls:** dead-ball, double, flagrant, live-ball, multiple, nonplayer/
  unsportsmanlike, player, **post-scrimmage kick (PSK)**, simultaneous with the
  snap. **No foul causes loss of the ball, and no foul makes a live ball dead.**
- **Spots** that drive enforcement: previous, dead-ball, inbounds, out-of-bounds,
  succeeding, spot of the foul, **post-scrimmage kick spot**, end of the run.
- **Loose-ball play vs running play (2-33)** — the distinction that selects the
  basic spot in Rule 10.
- **Neutral zone:** the ball's length at scrimmage, 10 yards on a free kick; may
  expand up to 2 yards behind the defensive line after the snap (never into the
  end zone on a scrimmage kick).

### Rule 3 — Periods, Time Factors and Substitutions

- **Coin toss** 3 minutes before kickoff (≤4 captains in uniform); winner may take
  a first-half option or **defer**; teams change goals after the 1st and 3rd
  periods.
- **Ending a period:** if time expires during a down, play it out. The period is
  **extended by an untimed down** for an accepted penalty, a double foul, an
  inadvertent whistle, or a touchdown needing a try — but **not** for
  unsportsmanlike or nonplayer fouls, loss-of-down fouls, fouls enforced on the
  kickoff, or fouls whose enforcement is a safety.
- **Time-outs:** three charged per team per half (1 minute; unused ones do not
  carry over, and second-half time-outs do not carry into overtime). Requests
  after they are gone are granted only for injury, equipment repair or a rules
  review. A **coach-referee conference** to review a possible misapplication stays
  charged unless the ruling changes.
- **Official's time-outs** include measurements, first downs, change of
  possession, injury, **concussion signs (player out until cleared by a health-care
  professional)**, **blood/open wound**, helmet coming completely off, and missing
  or improperly worn equipment — each of those last four **replaces the player for
  at least one down**.
- **Play clock: 40 seconds** normally, starting when the ball is declared dead;
  **25 seconds** on the ready-for-play after a score/try, to start a period, after
  a charged or official's time-out, after an inadvertent whistle, and after a legal
  kick that awards a new series.
- **Clock stops** on: foul, official's/charged time-out, end of period, out of
  bounds, incomplete pass, score or touchback, fair catch, inadvertent whistle,
  airborne receiver carried out of bounds. **Inside the last 2 minutes of a half,
  the offended team may choose to start the clock on the snap.** The referee may
  fix obvious game-clock errors before the second live ball, and play-clock errors
  before the snap.
- **Substitutions:** replaced players begin leaving **within 3 seconds** and exit
  on their own team-box side; no in-and-out during the same dead-ball interval;
  entering subs must be on their own side of the neutral zone at the snap.
  Illegal substitution is 5 yards — dead-ball, live-ball or nonplayer depending on
  which article was broken; *participating* is illegal participation (15).
- **Delay of game** (5 yards) covers the play clock expiring, snapping before the
  ready, carrying the ball away, failing to unpile, and other stalling. Failing to
  play within 2 minutes of the referee's order is a **forfeit**.

### Rule 4 — Ball in Play, Dead Ball and Out of Bounds

- Kickoff starts each half and follows a field goal or try; free kick also follows
  a safety, a replayed free-kick down, and a fair catch/awarded fair catch. Snaps
  and free kicks are made **between the hash marks**.
- **Ball becomes dead** when: the runner is down, out of bounds or his progress is
  stopped; a pass is incomplete; a kick breaks R's goal-line plane or a scoring
  attempt fails; opponents catch/recover simultaneously; a loose ball is motionless
  and unattended; K catches or recovers (as specified); any score;
  **the runner's helmet comes completely off**; a prosthetic limb comes off.
  Place-kick holder exceptions keep the ball live when a knee-down holder handles
  the snap.
- **Inadvertent whistle:** during a kick or a pass/snap in flight → replay the
  down; during a loose ball → last team in possession chooses replay or the ball
  where possession was lost; in player possession → that team takes the dead-ball
  spot or a replay. An accepted live-ball penalty from before the whistle takes
  precedence.
- **Out of bounds:** the spot is where the ball's foremost point crossed the
  sideline; a pylon means out of bounds behind the goal line. **New for 2025:** a
  forward fumble out of bounds between the goal lines returns to the **spot of the
  fumble**.

### Rule 5 — Series of Downs and Down After Penalty

- Ball dead between the hash marks → play from there; dead in a side zone → the
  **nearest hash mark** (incomplete legal passes and inadvertent-whistle replays
  return to the previous spot). A may pick any spot between the hash marks for a
  try, kickoff, free kick after a safety, fair catch, touchback and overtime
  series.
- **New series** to A on or beyond the line to gain; to B whenever B has the ball
  at the end of a down or A is short after fourth down; to the team in possession
  after a change of possession; to R on kicks per 5-1-3e–g. Each first down
  restarts the count; **no series ever starts on a down other than first.**
- Line to gain is set **after** all fouls before the ready-for-play are enforced,
  then stays fixed for the series. Measurements are parallel to the sideline; the
  referee may deny an obvious one.
- After a penalty, the ball belongs to the team in possession at the time of the
  foul unless it was a **PSK foul or kick-catching interference**. **Loss of down
  is meaningless after a change of possession or if the line to gain is reached.**

### Rule 6 — Kicking the Ball and Fair Catch

- **Free-kick lines are always 10 yards apart:** K's 40 for a kickoff, its 20 after
  a safety, the yard line of the catch after a fair catch. Only the kicker and
  holder may be beyond the line; **no other K player more than 5 yards behind it**;
  **at least four K players on each side of the kicker**; a punt may be used only
  after a safety; **pop-up kicks are illegal** (5 yards).
- **First touching** by K before the ball crosses R's free-kick line (or, on a
  scrimmage kick, beyond the expanded neutral zone) lets R take the ball at that
  spot — a right that is **cancelled if R then fouls** or if any penalty from the
  down is accepted. First touching is always ignored when a penalty is accepted.
- K may recover a free kick once it has **both** touched the ground and gone beyond
  R's free-kick line (either order), or after any legal R touch. A recovered kick
  is dead — K may never advance a free kick.
- **Scrimmage kicks** may be kicked only from in or behind the neutral zone before
  a change of possession. Beyond the neutral zone, only R may advance. Touching of
  a low kick in or behind the expanded neutral zone is ignored.
- **Free kick out of bounds untouched by R** (5 yards): R may take a re-kick 5
  yards back, 5 yards from the succeeding spot, or the ball 25 yards beyond the
  previous spot when that spot is on the midfield side of R's 25.
- **Fair catch:** a valid signal is a full-arm lateral wave above the head. Only
  the signaler is protected; a catch by a teammate still kills the ball. After any
  valid **or invalid** signal, no receiver may advance. Signaling forfeits blocking
  until the kick ends (15 yards).
- **Kick-catching interference** (15 yards): K may not touch the ball or R, or
  obstruct R's path, while a kickable kick is in flight — even with no signal
  given. R chooses the result of the play, an **awarded fair catch** after a
  15-yard penalty from the spot of the foul, or 15 yards from the previous spot and
  a replay. K *may* catch or bat a scrimmage kick in flight beyond the neutral zone
  when no R player is in position to catch it.
- **Touchback** on any non-scoring kick breaking R's goal-line plane, or a failed
  three-point attempt; ball at the 20.

### Rule 7 — Snapping, Handling and Passing the Ball

- **Snap:** quick, continuous, backward, immediately leaving the snapper's hands
  and touching a back or the ground before an A lineman. After touching the ball
  the snapper may not remove both hands, simulate a snap, or fail to pause. An
  illegal snap leaves the ball dead (5).
- **Encroachment** (5): after the ready-for-play, breaking the neutral-zone plane,
  contacting the ball before the snap ends, or contacting the snapper's hands/arms.
  **False start** (5): shift or feigned charge simulating the snap, an act intended
  to draw B in, or a hand movement by an interior lineman. A false start that draws
  B in penalizes only the false start. **Disconcerting acts** by B: 5 yards.
- **Formation:** every A player and substitute must have been momentarily between
  the 9-yard marks; **at least five A players on the line numbered 50–79**; at most
  four backs; only one player may break the waist plane of his nearest lineman.
  Scrimmage-kick-formation exceptions let a 0–49/80–99 snapper play (four 50–79
  linemen required, and that player is an ineligible receiver). After a shift or
  huddle, **all 11 stop for at least one full second**; only one player may be in
  motion at the snap and never toward the opponent's goal (5 yards each).
- **Handing:** backward any time; forward only before a change of possession, from
  in or behind the neutral zone, and only to a back or to a lineman who has
  half-turned and is 1 yard behind the line (5 + loss of down).
- **Forward pass:** one per down, thrown with both feet in or behind the neutral
  zone, before a change of possession. Illegal passes: after a change of
  possession (5), from beyond the neutral zone or a second pass (5 + loss of down),
  **intentional grounding** (5 + loss of down) — with the two spike/throwaway
  exceptions (immediately after a clean snap by the player behind the snapper; and
  the passer outside the free-blocking-zone boundary throwing to or past the
  neutral zone).
- **Eligibility:** A players on the ends of the line or legally behind it and
  numbered 0–49/80–99 (up to six), all B players, and all A players once B touches
  the pass. Eligible at the start of the down = eligible all down.
- **Pass interference** (15) exists only beyond the neutral zone, on a legal pass
  that crosses it untouched by B; restrictions start at the snap for A and at the
  release for B and end when the pass is touched or incomplete. Not interference on
  simultaneous bona-fide attempts, immediate contact by A on a B lineman that stops
  at the expanded neutral zone, or B contact away from the pass.
- **Ineligible downfield** 5 yards (previous spot); **illegal touching by an
  ineligible** 5 + loss of down (basic spot).

### Rule 8 — Scoring Plays and Touchbacks

- Possession of a live ball in the opponent's end zone is **always** a touchdown.
- **Try** from any spot between the hash marks on B's 3-yard line — untimed,
  unnumbered. 2 points for a touchdown, 1 for a field goal or safety; **only A may
  score.** Skipped after a last-play touchdown that cannot affect the outcome or
  playoff qualifying. The opponent of the scoring team chooses who kicks off.
- Fouls by the opponent during or after a scoring play (and unsportsmanlike or
  nonplayer fouls by either team) may be enforced **on the try or on the subsequent
  kickoff** — the scoring team's choice. A loss-of-down foul by A during a
  successful try wipes out the score with no replay.
- **Field goal:** place kick or drop kick from scrimmage or after a fair catch;
  must clear the crossbar between the uprights without touching the ground or a K
  player beyond the expanded neutral zone. A kickoff or free kick after a safety
  can never score a field goal.
- **Safety** when a runner takes the ball from the field of play into his own end
  zone and it dies in his team's possession, when a player forces a loose ball
  across his own goal line, or when a penalty against A is enforced from its end
  zone. **Momentum exception:** an interception/recovery/kick catch between the
  5-yard line and the goal line whose momentum carries the player into the end zone
  belongs to his team at the spot of the catch — now including a fumble that goes
  from the end zone into the field of play and out of bounds.
- **Touchback:** kicks breaking R's goal-line plane, failed three-point attempts,
  a pass intercepted and dead in B's end zone, and the force-based cases in 8-5-3.
  Ball in play at the 20 (snap after a touchback, free kick after a safety).

### Rule 9 — Conduct of Players and Others

- **5 yards:** helping the runner (push/pull/lift).
- **10 yards:** illegal blocking technique, interlocked blocking, holding, illegal
  use of hands, runner grasping a teammate, **block in the back**, illegal block on
  a free kick (K may not block until the kick has gone 10 yards, K is eligible to
  recover, or R blocks first inside the neutral zone), illegal kicking or batting.
- **15 yards:** blocking below the waist outside the free-blocking zone, blocking
  after a fair-catch signal, blocking the free kicker or holder, clipping, chop
  block, and the personal fouls of **9-4-3** — swinging a foot/knee, charging into
  a player out of the play or after the ball is dead, piling on, hurdling,
  face-mask twist (incidental grasp is 5), **illegal helmet contact**,
  **targeting**, **horse-collar** (enforced as a live-ball foul), **illegal
  blindside block**, tripping, striking/kicking/kneeing (also disqualification),
  contact with a helmet-less opponent, and **forceful contact against a defenseless
  receiver (9-4-3p)** unless it is incidental while playing the ball, initiated
  with open hands, or a wrap-up tackle attempt.
- **Automatic first down:** roughing the passer, roughing the kicker or holder,
  roughing the snapper (15 yards; running into the kicker without displacement is
  only 5). Simply *running into* ≠ roughing.
- **Unsportsmanlike conduct** (15): baiting/taunting, profanity or vulgar gestures,
  prolonged self-focused acts, kicking at the ball, spiking or throwing the ball
  after it is dead, refusing an official's request, tobacco/alcohol/impairment from
  arrival until departure. **A second unsportsmanlike foul = disqualification**;
  flagrant acts are immediate disqualification.
- **Illegal participation** (15, basic spot): leaving the field and returning,
  12+ players participating, deceptive substitutions, a player lying down to
  deceive, a disqualified player returning, or continuing to play after the helmet
  comes off.
- **Nonplayers/coaches (9-8):** unsportsmanlike acts, arguing calls, unauthorized
  conferences, illegal communication equipment, being outside the team box, not
  being ready to start a half, allowing illegal equipment — 15 yards, several
  charged to the head coach. **Sideline interference** escalates: warning → 5 yards
  → 15 yards. A substitute leaving the box during a fight is 15 + disqualification.
  A disqualified coach must leave the stadium area with no further team contact.
- **Unfair acts (9-9):** the referee may impose any equitable penalty **including
  awarding a score**; repeated half-the-distance fouls can forfeit the game.

### Rule 10 — Enforcement of Penalties

- Penalties are **5, 10 or 15 yards**, always declinable, and **never more than
  half the distance** to the offender's goal line. Choices are final once made and
  must be made before any charged time-out.
- **Order of enforcement:** a live-ball foul followed by a dead-ball foul by the
  opponent is penalized separately, in order. Multiple live-ball fouls by one team
  → the offended team picks one. Equal numbers of 15-yard unsportsmanlike/
  nonplayer/dead-ball personal fouls by both teams offset; the remainder are
  enforced in order.
- **Double foul** → penalties cancel, replay the down. But if possession changed
  and the team that ended up with the ball fouled only *after* the change, it may
  **keep the ball** by declining its opponent's penalty (same idea for R when all
  of R's fouls are PSK fouls).
- **Basic spots** — the heart of the chapter:

  | Situation | Basic spot |
  | --- | --- |
  | Foul simultaneous with the snap/free kick | Previous spot |
  | Foul behind the line during a loose-ball play | Previous spot |
  | Run/related run ends behind the line, no change of possession | Previous spot |
  | Foul by A behind the line, run ends beyond the line (or vice versa) | Previous spot |
  | Foul by B when the run ends beyond the line | End of the run |
  | Foul by A beyond the line, behind the end of the run | Spot of the foul |
  | Foul beyond the end of the run after a change of possession | End of the run |
  | R's post-scrimmage kick foul | Where the kick ended (PSK spot) |
  | Illegal batting/kicking, second-or-later forward pass, intentional grounding, A's foul in its own end zone | Spot of the foul |
  | Unsportsmanlike, nonplayer or dead-ball foul; touchback after a change of possession | Succeeding spot |
  | Force-into-the-end-zone cases of 10-4-7 | The 20-yard line |

- **Special enforcement:** free kick out of bounds, kick-catching interference,
  unfair acts, fouls on successful tries/field goals, fouls during or after a
  touchdown, roughing the passer/kicker/snapper, and K's fouls during a kick
  (offended team may choose succeeding-spot enforcement).
- An accepted penalty for a foul by A during a scoring play **nullifies the score**
  (unsportsmanlike/nonplayer fouls excepted). No penalty *directly* produces a
  safety, but enforcement from behind A's own goal line does.

## 5. Fundamentals worth memorizing

Straight from FOOTBALL FUNDAMENTALS — the
principles that resolve most arguments:

1. A live ball is always in some team's possession; **no foul causes loss of the
   ball**, and no live-ball foul makes the ball dead.
2. A player cannot fumble before gaining possession, and **possession in the
   opponent's end zone is always a touchdown**.
3. The whistle rarely kills the ball — it is already dead by rule. A dead ball
   becomes live only by a legal snap or free kick.
4. A kick ends the instant a player secures possession. Any kick that is not a
   scoring attempt is dead when it breaks R's goal-line plane.
5. After any valid **or invalid** fair-catch signal by any R player, no receiver may
   advance.
6. **Only three defensive fouls give an automatic first down:** roughing the
   kicker/holder, the passer, or the snapper.
7. Pass interference can only happen **beyond** the neutral zone. A player eligible
   at the start of the down stays eligible all down. A handed ball is not a pass.
8. A live-ball foul plus a later dead-ball foul is never a double foul; a foul
   during a try never pairs with a dead-ball foul either.
9. There can be several running plays in a down, each with its own basic spot, but
   **only one loose-ball play**.
10. Any distance penalty may be declined; all penalties are 5, 10 or 15 yards; each
    is enforced from the basic spot unless a rule says otherwise.

## 6. Overtime (state-adopted procedure)

From RESOLVING TIED GAMES — a model procedure each
state may adopt, amend or reject.

- 3-minute intermission, coin toss, and officials review carry-over penalties.
- Each team gets one series, **first and goal from the 10** (15 in six-player),
  from any spot between the hash marks, at the same end of the field.
- **The line to gain is always the goal line.** A field-goal attempt is allowed on
  any down. Each team gets **one time-out per overtime period**.
- The offensive series ends on any score or the moment the defense gains
  possession. **A defensive safety or touchdown ends the game.** An offensive
  safety puts the other team's series at the 10 (temporary 2–0).
- Ties repeat with 2-minute intermissions; the loser of the toss chooses first in
  the second period, then options alternate. **PSK enforcement does not apply.**
- The 17 interpretations (3.1.1 SITUATION A–Q) cover carry-over penalties, fouls
  after a score, and defensive possession ending a series.

## 7. Nine-, eight- and six-player differences

From NINE-, EIGHT- AND SIX-PLAYER RULES DIFFERENCES.
Eleven-player rules apply except:

| | Nine-player | Eight-player | Six-player |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Field | 80 × 40 yards, 7-yard marks | 80 × 40 yards, 7-yard marks | 80 × 40 yards, 7-yard marks |
| Free-blocking zone | 3 yards each side | 3 yards each side | 3 yards each side |
| Free-kick lines | K's 30 / R's 40 | K's 30 / R's 40 | K's 30 / R's 40 |
| Players beside the kicker | 3 each side | 3 each side | 2 each side |
| Line at the snap | 3 players, any numbers | 3 players, any numbers | 3 players, any numbers |
| Eligible receivers | 2 ends + 4 backs | 2 ends + 3 backs | All (pass caught by the passer is incomplete) |
| Line to gain | 10 yards | 10 yards | **15 yards** |
| Periods | 12 minutes | 12 minutes | **10 minutes** |
| Touchback/safety spot | 20 | **15** | **15** |
| Scoring | Standard | Standard | **Field goal 4**; try 2 by kick, 1 by run/pass |
| Special | — | — | Ball may not cross the line of scrimmage in possession until possession changes from the snap receiver; overtime starts at the 15 |

Conferences use the 7-yard marks in all three codes, and the 10-4-7 basic spot
becomes the 15-yard line.
